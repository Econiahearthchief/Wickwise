Best wax for candle making — soy vs. paraffin vs. beeswax vs. coconut

There's no single "best" wax — there's a best wax for your priorities. This breaks each one down by what actually differs: how it burns, how it holds scent, how hard it is to work with, and where it tends to go wrong.

Soy wax

Fill factor: 0.86 · Max fragrance load: 10% · Burn rate: ~6 hrs/oz

The default choice for a reason — clean-burning, renewable, forgiving to work with, and a good middle ground on both cold and hot throw. Its main weakness is a lower melting point, which means soy candles are more prone to frosting (a natural white, crystalline surface texture) and can develop wet spots in containers if the room temperature swings. Neither is a defect — frosting is cosmetic, not a burn problem — but it surprises first-time makers who expect a perfectly glassy surface every time.

Best for: beginners, eco-conscious branding, container candles.

Paraffin wax

Fill factor: 0.90 · Max fragrance load: 12% · Burn rate: ~4.5 hrs/oz

The traditional choice, and still the strongest for hot throw — paraffin releases fragrance more aggressively once burning than any other wax on this list, which is why it dominates in votives and heavily-scented commercial candles. It also takes color more vibrantly. The tradeoff is a faster burn rate (meaning less total burn time per ounce) and it's petroleum-based, which matters if your brand leans natural or eco-focused.

Best for: strongest scent throw, votives, vibrant colors.

Beeswax

Fill factor: 0.95 · Max fragrance load: 6% · Burn rate: ~7 hrs/oz

The slowest burning and longest-lasting wax here, with a naturally sweet, honey-like scent of its own — which is exactly why it takes added fragrance the least well of the four. Pushing past its 6% ceiling is the fastest way to get seeping with this wax specifically. It's also the most expensive and has natural air-purifying properties often used in marketing. Many makers use it unscented or very lightly scented rather than fighting its natural character.

Best for: natural/unscented candles, longest burn time, premium positioning.

Coconut wax

Fill factor: 0.85 · Max fragrance load: 10% · Burn rate: ~6.5 hrs/oz

The newest mainstream option and increasingly popular for "luxury" branding — coconut wax has excellent cold throw, a smooth, creamy finish, and burns cleanly. It's rarely sold pure, though; most "coconut wax" candles are actually a coconut/soy blend, since pure coconut wax is soft and can struggle to hold its shape without a container. Worth checking your supplier's exact blend ratio before assuming you're working with 100% coconut.

Best for: luxury positioning, slow burn, smooth finish.

Soy/paraffin blends

Fill factor: 0.88 · Max fragrance load: 10% · Burn rate: ~5.5 hrs/oz

A practical middle ground — blends aim to capture soy's clean burn and easier handling alongside paraffin's stronger hot throw. Ratios vary by maker (a common starting point is 70% soy / 30% paraffin), and the right ratio depends on which quality you're trying to lean toward. This is also the wax type most worth test-burning carefully, since a blend's real-world fragrance ceiling and ideal wick size can differ from either parent wax on its own — don't assume it splits the difference evenly.

Best for: balancing throw strength against clean-burn branding.

Side-by-side

Wax Max load Burn rate Cure time Difficulty
Soy 10% 6 hr/oz 1–2 weeks Easy
Paraffin 12% 4.5 hr/oz 24–48 hrs Easy
Beeswax 6% 7 hr/oz ~2 weeks Moderate
Coconut 10% 6.5 hr/oz 1–2 weeks Moderate
Blend 10% 5.5 hr/oz 1–2 weeks Moderate

If you're choosing your first wax and don't have a strong reason to pick otherwise, soy remains the easiest entry point — it's the most forgiving of measurement mistakes and has the most supplier data and community troubleshooting available if something goes wrong.
